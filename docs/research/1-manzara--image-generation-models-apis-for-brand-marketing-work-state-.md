## Özet

By August 2026 the image-gen market has split into three usable layers. (1) Frontier multimodal "reasoning image" models — Google's Nano Banana family (gemini-3-pro-image at $0.134/1K-2K, $0.24/4K; gemini-3.1-flash-image at $0.045–$0.151), OpenAI gpt-image-2 (up to 3840px, 16 reference images, $0.006–$0.211 depending on quality), and ByteDance Seedream 5.0 Pro (~$0.045–$0.0675). These win on prompt adherence, multi-reference character consistency (Nano Banana Pro holds 5 characters + 14 objects) and legible in-image text. (2) Controllable/design-native models — BFL FLUX.2 (klein 4B $0.014 → max $0.07, megapixel-priced, up to 10 refs, 4MP, exact hex color control), Ideogram 4.0/P-Image (with real API-based custom model *training*), Recraft V4.1 (only mainstream true-SVG output, $0.035 raster → $0.30 pro vector), and Sourceful Riverflow 2.5 (from $0.13/img, uniquely accepts **custom font files** and a reasoning-effort dial). (3) Open weights you can actually self-host commercially — FLUX.2 [klein] 4B (Apache-2.0), Qwen-Image-2512 / Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 (Apache-2.0), Z-Image-Turbo 6B (Apache-2.0, 16GB VRAM), HiDream-I1 (MIT). Notably FLUX.2 [dev] and [klein] 9B are NOT commercially self-hostable without a paid BFL licence.
Aggregators matter more than ever: OpenRouter now has a real `/api/v1/images` endpoint with 43 image models behind one key; fal.ai has the deepest catalogue plus LoRA training; Freepik has rebranded to Magnific and ships an official MCP server, as do BFL and Ideogram. Imagen 4 is being shut down on 2026-08-17. Midjourney still has NO public API and its ToS forbids automation. Truly free tiers are nearly gone: Cloudflare Workers AI (10,000 neurons/day) and Pollinations.ai (now SANA-only) are the only zero-cost programmatic paths.

### Öneriler

- Build a three-tier routing policy today and encode it as config, not code: DRAFT tier = Z-Image Turbo on fal ($0.005/MP) or Cloudflare's free 10,000-neuron/day FLUX.2 klein for exploring 20-50 angles; MID tier = Seedream 4.5 ($0.04 flat for 4MP with 14 refs) or gemini-3.1-flash-image ($0.067/1K) for shortlisted candidates; HERO tier = gemini-3-pro-image ($0.134) or FLUX.2 [flex] for the one asset that actually ships. At realistic solo volume this keeps your monthly image spend under $20.
- Migrate any Imagen 4 usage immediately — Google is shutting down imagen-4.0-generate-001, -ultra and -fast on 2026-08-17, three days from now. Replace with gemini-3.1-flash-image, which is cheaper at 1K and strictly better.
- Do NOT trust any model to render Turkish headline text reliably. Adopt a composite pipeline as your default for social posts: generate a clean text-free background with the image model, then overlay Turkish copy as real vector text using a font you control (Satori/resvg, sharp, or ImageMagick over an SVG template). This guarantees correct ğ, ı, İ, ş, ç, ö and ü every single time, gives you exact brand typography, and lets you regenerate copy without regenerating the image. Use in-model text rendering only for decorative or background lettering.
- Where you do want the model to render text, prioritise Sourceful Riverflow 2.5 Pro — it is the only API found that accepts a custom font file, which is precisely the mechanism that fixes Turkish diacritics. Budget $0.13/image and use it only for hero typographic posters; benchmark it head-to-head against Nano Banana Pro on a Turkish test string before committing.
- Standardise your integration on OpenRouter's /api/v1/images endpoint as the primary router and fal.ai as the secondary. OpenRouter gives you 43 models behind one key with a uniform parameter schema (resolution, aspect_ratio, n, input_references, seed) so your agent can swap models by changing a string; fal covers what OpenRouter lacks (LoRA training, FLUX Fill/Erase/Outpaint, Ideogram v4, Z-Image) with the clearest published prices in the industry.
- Spend $3 on a brand-style LoRA before spending anything else on prompt engineering. fal's krea-2-trainer is $0.003/step (1,000 steps = $3.00) and Krea 2 Medium Turbo inference is then $0.015/image; alternatively train a FLUX.2 klein LoRA locally for free with ostris/ai-toolkit on the Apache-2.0 klein-base-4B checkpoint. A trained LoRA is a far more durable brand-consistency mechanism than any per-request style-reference parameter.
- For your logo and icon system, use Recraft V4.1 Vector as the single source of truth — it is the only mainstream API that returns genuinely editable SVG, which means you can recolour to exact brand hex values programmatically and scale to any output without regeneration. Do not let a raster model draw your logo; composite the real SVG in afterwards.
- Connect the three first-party MCP servers (BFL FLUX, Ideogram, Magnific) into your Claude Code environment rather than writing HTTP clients. All three use OAuth with no API-key handling, and Magnific's exposes 40+ tools including character training and asset/folder management — which is most of your Creative Suite asset layer for free.
- Keep the entire self-hosted path Apache-2.0: run Qwen-Image-2512 plus Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 and FLUX.2 [klein] 4B on ComfyUI, exposed headlessly via POST /prompt with templated workflow JSON. Explicitly avoid self-hosting FLUX.2 [dev] and FLUX.2 [klein] 9B — those weights are under the FLUX Non-Commercial Licence and serving them commercially requires a paid BFL agreement, even though their outputs are freely usable.
- Write Ideogram's URL-expiry behaviour into your pipeline as a hard rule: their API returns time-limited image links, so every generation handler must download and persist bytes to your own storage in the same request cycle. The same discipline protects you across every provider and gives you the asset library your Creative Suite needs.
- Treat Midjourney as unavailable. It has no official API in August 2026 and its guidelines prohibit automated interaction and third-party apps; every 'Midjourney API' relay drives a real account and risks termination. Krea 2 Large ($0.060/image, with a first-party trainer) is the legitimate substitute for its aesthetic.
- Benchmark once, then freeze: build a 10-prompt Turkish evaluation set (headline poster, product shot with logo, carousel slide, Reels cover, LinkedIn banner) and run it across gemini-3-pro-image, FLUX.2 flex, Riverflow 2.5 Pro, Seedream 5.0 Pro and Ideogram 4.0. Vendor claims about multilingual text quality are marketing; a $5 bake-off gives you the only answer that matters for your language.


### Riskler

- Turkish text rendering is essentially unproven. Google claims text error rates 'mostly under 10% across languages' and Seedream 5.0 Pro is reported to write 14 languages natively, but no vendor publishes a Turkish-specific figure, and the strongest open models (Qwen-Image, Z-Image) advertise English+Chinese only. Turkish-specific glyphs (dotless ı, dotted İ, ğ, ş) are exactly the characters diffusion models mangle. Assume in-model Turkish text will fail until you personally verify it.
- Model deprecation is now fast and frequent. Imagen 4 dies 2026-08-17, gpt-image-1 dies 2026-10-23, DALL·E 2/3 were removed 2026-05-12. Any hardcoded model ID is a future outage. Put model IDs in configuration with a documented fallback chain and a monthly review.
- The FLUX open-weights licence split is a live legal trap: klein-4B and klein-base-4B are Apache-2.0, but klein-9B and dev are FLUX Non-Commercial. If the Creative Suite ever stops being 'personal use for now' — a client project, a paid newsletter, anything commercial — self-hosting those checkpoints becomes a licence breach requiring a paid BFL agreement.
- ComfyUI is GPL-3.0. Bundling and distributing it inside a product triggers copyleft obligations. SwarmUI's MIT licence does not cure this because it auto-installs the GPL ComfyUI backend. If productisation is even a distant possibility, prefer InvokeAI (Apache-2.0) as the self-hosted base.
- Per-image pricing is increasingly not per-image. FLUX.2 bills by megapixel on BOTH input references and output, gpt-image-2 bills by tokens, Riverflow prices dynamically after completion, and Cloudflare bills per 512px tile per step. A pipeline that sends 8 reference images at 4MP each can cost 10x your estimate. Instrument actual spend per call from day one — Reve and OpenRouter both return credits-used in the response, so log it.
- Truly free tiers have almost disappeared. Google explicitly lists 'Free tier: Not available' for every image model. Pollinations has collapsed to a single low-quality model (SANA). Only Cloudflare's 10,000 neurons/day remains meaningful, and Cloudflare could reprice it. Do not architect around free inference.
- Chinese-vendor models (Seedream, Qwen Image 3, HunyuanImage) offer the best price/quality but route your brand assets, product photography and unreleased campaign material through PRC-affiliated infrastructure. Regional availability from Turkey and data-handling terms should be checked before sending anything confidential.
- Aggregator prices drift from source-of-truth prices and sometimes disagree: Seedream 5.0 Pro shows 'from $0.045' on OpenRouter but '$0.0675 tentative' on fal, and Recraft's own credit units (40/80) do not reconcile with the credit numbers shown in its web-app pricing. Verify against the primary vendor before building a cost model, and re-verify quarterly.
- Every Google image output carries a SynthID watermark and OpenAI images carry C2PA metadata. This is harmless for honest marketing but means all such assets are permanently machine-identifiable as AI-generated — relevant if a platform, marketplace or future client requires human-authored provenance.
- Automated posting is a separate ToS surface from generation. Nothing found in the image-generation terms blocks automation, but the destination platforms (Instagram, LinkedIn, X) each impose their own automation and API rules. Generating with a compliant model does not make scheduled posting compliant — that must be validated per platform.
- Ideogram image URLs expire and are not archival. Several other providers return presigned URLs with short TTLs (Adobe returns presigned S3 links). Losing generated assets to link expiry is a mundane but real failure mode for an asset-library-centric system.
- Vendor concentration risk in the aggregator layer: if you route everything through OpenRouter or fal and either has an outage, changes margins, or drops a model, your entire pipeline stalls. Keep at least one direct vendor key (BFL or Google) configured as an escape hatch.
