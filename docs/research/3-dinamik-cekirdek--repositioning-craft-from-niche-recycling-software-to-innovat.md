## Özet

Research finding, blunt: there is no published, adopted machine-readable schema for marketing/positioning strategy. GitHub search returns only agent-skill packs (arnabbagxd/Brand-building-skills, 514★ MIT, active June 2026; cofoundy/brand-skills; niranjanbala/brand-marketing-frameworks) which are prose markdown with YAML frontmatter, not typed data. schema.org gives vocabulary for Organization/Brand/Offer/OfferCatalog but nothing for positioning, ICP, proof or objections. So Upcytech's schema must be invented — the good news is the source frameworks are already field-shaped and I give a concrete design below.

Which frameworks yield structured data rather than prose: April Dunford's five components (competitive alternatives → unique attributes → value → target segment → market category) are effectively five typed relational tables with foreign keys — best backbone, and verified current: Obviously Awesome 2nd edition ships in 2026 with a new pre-work section and a new chapter on value. Blue Ocean's Strategy Canvas (factors × offering level, per-competitor value curves) and the ERRC grid are literally numeric arrays. Ulwick/ODI desired-outcome statements have fixed syntax. Value Proposition Canvas is six typed lists. Play Bigger, StoryBrand and messaging house are narrative shapes — encode them as derived views, not as source of truth.

Dunford (Feb 2026, "Positioning in the Age of AI") names exactly three repositioning triggers — product change, competitor change, market change — which is your re-run trigger enum. She also separates value from objection handling: they must be different entity types, because objections attach to gatekeeper personas and never win a deal.

For the hybrid problem, the credible 2026 model is Palantir's forward-deployed engineer: bespoke configuration on a productised platform, with an explicit path from field work into product ("some of our most valuable product additions originated in the field"). Brand-architecture analogue for Upcytech + dima: 37signals — parent as philosophy/authority brand, independently named products.

Anti-AI-washing has legal teeth (FTC Operation AI Comply; SEC v. Delphia/Global Predictions, $400k) and buyer teeth: 2026 State of Marketing to Engineers finds AI tools least-trusted source (10%), vendor engineers most-trusted authors (66%), 69% using genAI to buy anyway.

### Riskler

- No adoptable standard exists. Every search returned either prose agent-skills or nothing. This means the schema below is bespoke and unvalidated by any community — budget for two or three painful revisions in the first year as real content generation exposes missing fields. Mitigation: keep the typed core tiny and push everything speculative into the schema-driven `attributes` object, so revisions are data migrations rather than code changes.
- Over-schematisation is the dominant failure mode. A one-person company can easily spend three months building a strategy CMS and zero weeks talking to manufacturers. The positioning that matters is discovered in sales conversations, not in JSON. Hard rule: do not build entity types 6-15 until entity types 1-5 have produced at least ten real published assets and one closed deal.
- Regeneration can silently destroy institutional memory. If a re-run overwrites rather than supersedes, the founder loses the reasoning behind decisions he may want to reverse. The immutability + supersedes + migration_map design is not optional decoration — it is the only thing standing between 'run it again' and self-inflicted amnesia.
- Agents will conflate value with objection handling by default. LLMs are trained to be comprehensive and will happily produce eight value themes, most of which are table stakes. Dunford's verified warning about 'listing too many value themes' and 'conflating differentiated value with sales objection handling' will manifest in your pipeline as bloated, undifferentiated decks unless the schema physically separates the two entity types and the linter enforces a cap.
- The AI-washing trap is live for exactly your positioning. Your stated differentiator — 'complex algorithmic AI/ML with high precision' — is phonetically identical to what every AI-washing vendor claims. Verified data: technical buyers rate AI tools the least trustworthy information source (10% very/extremely trustworthy, average 4.7/10) while 69% use generative AI to research purchases. A generated content pipeline producing AI-flavoured copy about AI capabilities is the maximum-suspicion configuration. The only escape is mechanism-level specificity, named human authorship, and published failure modes.
- Legal exposure is real, not hypothetical. FTC's Operation AI Comply and the SEC's $400k Delphia/Global Predictions actions establish that unsubstantiated AI capability claims are enforceable violations, and the FTC standard is that you must actually have conducted testing. An automated content generator that emits performance claims without a claim-registry gate is a compliance incident generator. UNVERIFIED: the equivalent Turkish and EU exposure — get local counsel to confirm before publishing numeric performance claims in Turkish.
- Repositioning while solo means the old niche keeps paying the bills. If recycling revenue funds the transition, a hard public break is financially reckless. The endorsed-brand plus bridge-narrative approach lets you keep serving recycling customers under the new frame ('recycling is the hardest manufacturing case') rather than forcing a choice. But watch for the failure mode where the bridge becomes an excuse never to complete the move — set a date by which manufacturing-native proof assets must exist.
- SEO and LLM-memory damage is slow and easy to miss. Verified Google guidance says redirects must be kept at least a year and re-indexing takes weeks; LLM caches are worse and less observable. Deleting recycling-era pages to 'clean up' the new identity is the single most common and most destructive rebranding mistake, because those pages hold your backlinks and your only third-party evidence.
- New-category temptation. Play Bigger's framing is seductive and a technically deep founder will be drawn to naming a new category. Category creation requires sustained market-education spend that a solo operator cannot fund, and it multiplies the education burden on every sales call. Dunford's 'big fish, small pond' — a well-defined existing category where you are clearly the leader for a narrow segment — is almost certainly the correct choice, and should be the schema's default.

The Value Proposition Canvas page states no Creative Commons or open-source licence (UNVERIFIED whether commercial reproduction is permitted); 'Blue Ocean Strategy' is trademarked; 'StoryBrand' and 'BrandScript' are trademarked.
