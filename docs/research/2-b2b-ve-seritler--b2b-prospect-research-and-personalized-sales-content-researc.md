## Özet

As of Aug 2026 the single most important structural fact is that the "buy LinkedIn data via API" era is over. Proxycurl is confirmed dead (nubela.co now says "Proxycurl is no longer in service", redirecting to NinjaPear), Crunchbase killed its Basic API ("We are no longer offering our Basic API"), and Google's Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers with a hard sunset of 1 Jan 2027. Meanwhile LinkedIn's User Agreement §8.2(4) bans using LinkedIn-derived data obtained "through third parties (such as ... data aggregators or brokers)" — so even buying a Bright Data LinkedIn dataset puts the buyer in breach. hiQ v. LinkedIn ended with hiQ losing on breach-of-contract despite winning on CFAA; scraping is not a crime but it is a contract violation with real account/legal consequences.

What replaces it for a solo operator is agentic open-web research: Exa ($7/1k searches, $10/mo free credits), Tavily (1,000 free credits/mo), Firecrawl (1,000 free credits/mo), Bright Data SERP (5,000 free requests/mo, then $1.50/1k). These are ToS-clean because they read the public web, not LinkedIn.

For deck generation the free lane is genuinely production-grade: Presenton (Apache-2.0, 9.5k stars) self-hosts, exposes `POST /api/v1/ppt/presentation/generate`, supports Ollama for zero-cost inference, takes a `language` param for Turkish, exports editable PPTX/PDF, and lets you define brand templates in HTML/Tailwind — meaning Upcytech's brand system becomes the deck template. Premium lane is Qwilr ($35/user/mo, API on all tiers).

Personalized video splits cleanly: Tavus gives 25 free CVI minutes/month with whitelabel API access on the free tier; HeyGen is pure pay-as-you-go wallet ($0.05–0.0667/sec, $1/avatar-creation call) with no subscription floor — ideal for a per-run cost estimate.

Turkey-specific programmatic company data is thin officially (KAP, MERSIS and Ticaret Sicil Gazetesi are all web-only, no API) but a strong Turkish OSS/MCP ecosystem exists — pykap, borsa-mcp, and notably ihale-mcp for government tenders, which is a real buying-intent signal.

<details><summary>Notlar</summary>

**Proxycurl (Nubela)** — Its disappearance is the clearest signal that LinkedIn-derived data-as-a-service is now a legally hostile category. Successor NinjaPear is unproven; treat as UNVERIFIED.

**LinkedIn User Agreement §8.2 (scraping prohibition)** — Verbatim clauses fetched: §8.2(2) bans 'software, devices, scripts, robots ... (such as crawlers, browser plugins and add-ons)' to scrape; §8.2(13) bans 'bots or other unauthorized automated methods to access the Services'; and critically §8.2(4) bans 'Copy, use, display or distribute any information ... obtained from the Services, whether directly or through third parties (such as search tools or data aggregators or brokers), without the consent of the content owner'. §8.2(4) is the one people miss — buying the data does not launder it.

**hiQ Labs v. LinkedIn (final outcome)** — 9th Cir. (2019, reaffirmed 2022) held scraping public data does not violate the CFAA; SCOTUS vacated once on Van Buren and remanded. But in 2022 N.D. Cal. found hiQ BREACHED LinkedIn's User Agreement, and the parties settled with hiQ effectively shut down. Net: CFAA-safe, contract-liable. For a Turkish solo operator the exposure is account termination plus civil claim, and KVKK/GDPR exposure on top.

**Exa (Search + Contents + Websets)** — Best fit for the 'research a named prospect' step because Websets does find+verify+enrich in one call, which is exactly the deck-input shape. Reads the open web, so no LinkedIn ToS entanglement.

**Tavily Search API** — Cheapest credible free tier of any search API surveyed and no card required, which matters given Turkish card/FX friction on US SaaS.

**Firecrawl** — For the free lane you can substitute Playwright (already installed in this environment) + trafilatura/readability locally at zero cost — slower and more brittle, but genuinely $0.

**Bright Data SERP API** — Bright Data also sells LinkedIn datasets. Use the SERP API; do NOT buy their LinkedIn people/company datasets — LinkedIn UA §8.2(4) makes broker-sourced LinkedIn data a breach for you as the consumer.

**Serper.dev** — Google Maps/Places data via Serper is a legitimate, ToS-clean way to profile Turkish SMEs that have no web presence beyond a Maps listing.

**Google Custom Search JSON API** — This invalidates a large amount of pre-2026 tutorial content that uses CSE as the default free search lane. Use Tavily or Bright Data SERP instead.

**Perplexity Sonar / Agent API** — The $0.005 people-search tool is a legal-ish substitute for the dead Proxycurl use case, but verify what source it draws from before putting personal data in a deck (KVKK).

**Apollo.io** — Much of Apollo's contact data is LinkedIn-derived. Using Apollo's COMPANY firmographics for deck personalization is low-risk; republishing individual LinkedIn-sourced profile detail is where §8.2(4) exposure lives. Prefer company-level fields.

**HubSpot Breeze Intelligence (ex-Clearbit)** — Standalone Clearbit is gone. Anyone recommending 'just use Clearbit's free 25 enrichments' is working from stale 2023 knowledge.

**Crunchbase API** — For Turkish funding signals, startup.gov.tr / TÜBİTAK / KOSGEB announcements and press coverage found via Tavily are more relevant than Crunchbase.

**Apify** — Apify hosts LinkedIn scraper actors. Running one puts YOU in breach of LinkedIn §8.2(2) — Apify's ToS pushes that liability to the actor's user. Do not use them.

**LinkedIn Marketing API (li-lms-2026-07)** — Important expectation-setting: there is no legal LinkedIn endpoint that turns a company name into a firmographic profile. Stop looking for one; use the open web instead.

**Clay** — Clay's real lesson for your build is the waterfall pattern: try free source → if null, try cheap source → if null, try premium source, and stop as soon as a field is filled. That IS your dual-lane cost model, and it should be a first-class abstraction in your Next.js command center, not a per-step toggle.

**KAP — Kamuyu Aydınlatma Platformu** — Only covers listed companies (~550), so it is useless for the typical Turkish SME prospect. Where it IS relevant it is gold: a KAP disclosure gives you audited revenue, headcount and strategy statements to quote in a deck.

**pykap** — MIT means no copyleft concern for your closed Creative Suite repo.

**saidsurucu/borsa-mcp + borsapy** — Being an MCP server it needs zero glue code — your agentic pipeline can call it as a tool directly.

**saidsurucu/ihale-mcp** — Build a pipeline step 'ihale signal → prospect dossier → tailored deck referencing their specific tender scope'. Nothing in the US-centric ABM stack gives you this for Turkey.

**saidsurucu/yargi-mcp + mevzuat-mcp** — Also useful for checking KVKK obligations on the prospect-data you store.

**Türkiye Ticaret Sicili Gazetesi** — A capital increase or new authorised signatory is a legitimate trigger event. Scraping it in bulk risks blocking; a single lookup for a named prospect before a meeting is proportionate and defensible.

**MERSİS (Merkezi Sicil Kayıt Sistemi)** — Store `mersis_no` and `vergi_no` as fields on your prospect record anyway — they are the canonical Turkish company identifiers and will be needed for proposals and invoicing regardless.

**Presenton** — Four properties make it the right answer for Upcytech: (1) custom templates authored in HTML + Tailwind, so your brand system in the repo BECOMES the deck template rather than being approximated; (2) a `language` parameter for Turkish output; (3) Ollama/LM Studio support so the free lane is genuinely $0 including inference; (4) exports fully editable PPTX so you can hand-tune before a meeting. **Marp / marp-cli** — marp-cli is the lowest-ceremony way to get deterministic PDF output from an agent, with themes as plain CSS.

**python-pptx** — The killer pattern for enterprise Turkish prospects: load THEIR template .pptx, inject your content into its layouts. Nothing signals 'tailored' harder. Presenton also does template-from-existing-PPTX, which may cover this without extra code.

**Gamma** — Even if it exists, the brand-fidelity argument favours Presenton/Slidev: you control the template, so every deck is unmistakably Upcytech.

**Qwilr** — View analytics ('the CFO opened slide 7 four times') is the thing OSS cannot give you and is genuinely worth money in a sales motion.

**PandaDoc API** — For Turkish B2B, e-signature needs to be checked against Turkish e-imza / KEP norms anyway — PandaDoc signatures may not carry the weight you want locally.

**Tavus** — 25 free minutes/month ≈ 12–25 prospect videos. Cost estimator formula: est_cost = max(0, minutes − remaining_quota) × 0.37. Verify Turkish is in the 30+ language set before committing — it was not enumerated on the pricing page.

**Vidyard** — Strongest as the HOSTING + view-analytics layer even if you generate video elsewhere — knowing the prospect watched 80% before the meeting changes how you open it.

**Bonjoro** — Counter-signal worth weighing: for high-value bespoke-software deals a real 60-second face-to-camera video in fluent Turkish outperforms an AI avatar. Consider a manual lane alongside the automated one — 'free lane' can mean 'you record it yourself'.

**Potion (sendpotion.com)** — Priced for SDR teams blasting sequences. Fundamentally the wrong shape for bespoke B2B.

**Loom API** — For the demo-video free lane, headless Playwright driving your actual product + ffmpeg screen capture gives you a real, honest product demo at $0 — arguably better than an avatar talking about the product.

**Remotion** — NOT an OSI open-source licence — it is source-available with commercial conditions. This is the licence footgun of this whole domain. The upside is enormous: same React brand components render your Instagram Reels, LinkedIn video AND prospect demo videos from one codebase, with the prospect's name/logo/data as props.

**Twenty CRM** — AGPL is fine for purely internal single-user use, but the moment you expose a modified instance over a network you owe source. Read the '@license Enterprise' file markers carefully before self-hosting in production.

**EspoCRM** — Same AGPL caveat as Twenty, but without the enterprise-file complication.

**Attio** — Free-tier API access is rare — HubSpot explicitly excludes free users from credits, PandaDoc gates API behind Enterprise, Bonjoro behind $399/mo. Attio giving it away at $0 for 3 seats makes it the obvious hosted choice.

**HubSpot Free CRM** — Its gravitational pull toward the paid tier is the real cost.

**Odoo CRM** — The One App Free plan with unlimited users at €0 is genuinely notable and under-known, but adopting Odoo means adopting Odoo's whole worldview.

**Markdown/JSON prospect records in the repo** — Decisive reasoning: when the CRM's only consumer is an agent, a database is a liability and a filesystem is an asset. ### Riskler

- LINKEDIN §8.2(4) IS THE TRAP MOST PEOPLE MISS — it forbids using LinkedIn-derived information obtained 'through third parties (such as search tools or data aggregators or brokers)'. Buying a Bright Data LinkedIn dataset or running an Apify LinkedIn actor puts YOU in breach even though you never scraped anything. Combined with hiQ (CFAA-safe but contract-liable, hiQ settled and shut down), the correct policy is a hard architectural ban: no LinkedIn scraping, no LinkedIn-broker datasets, no exceptions. Encode this as a lint rule over provider configs, not as a note in a README.
- VENDOR MORTALITY IS UNUSUALLY HIGH IN THIS CATEGORY — within this survey alone: Proxycurl terminated, Crunchbase Basic API withdrawn, Google Custom Search JSON API closed to new customers and dead 1 Jan 2027, Clearbit absorbed into HubSpot and paywalled, Loom's developer page 404ing. Any prospect-data provider must sit behind your own thin interface so it is swappable in an afternoon. Never let a provider's response schema leak into your deck templates.
- APOLLO'S API ENTITLEMENT IS CONTRADICTORY IN THEIR OWN DOCS — apollo.io/pricing says 'API access available on Custom plans only' while apollo.io/product/api implies broad availability. Verify inside your own account before writing a line of integration code. A pipeline built on assumed free-tier Apollo API access could be dead on arrival.
- REMOTION IS NOT OSI OPEN SOURCE — it is source-available with a Free License for individuals/small companies and a mandatory paid Company License above a headcount threshold that this research could NOT verify (the pricing component did not render). It is the highest-leverage tool in the stack (one React codebase for Reels, LinkedIn video and prospect demos) and therefore the most expensive licence mistake available. Confirm eligibility at remotion.pro in writing before building on it.
- AGPLv3 ON TWENTY AND ESPOCRM — fine for genuinely internal single-user use, but if you ever expose a modified instance over a network you owe source to its users. Twenty compounds this with files marked '@license Enterprise' that require a paid subscription for production deployment. For a company that SELLS software, casually adopting AGPL infrastructure is a risk worth avoiding entirely when markdown files do the job.
- TURKISH LANGUAGE QUALITY IS UNVERIFIED ACROSS THE ENTIRE VIDEO CATEGORY — HeyGen's page claims '175 languages & dialects' but did not enumerate Turkish; Tavus claims '30+ languages' without a list. Turkish TTS prosody is genuinely hard and a mispronounced company name or wrong stress pattern is worse than sending no video. Budget for one paid test render per vendor before any commitment.
- FREE TIERS ARE MONTHLY AND NON-ROLLING — Tavily 1,000/mo, Firecrawl 1,000/mo, Bright Data SERP 5,000/mo, Exa $10/mo, Apify $5/mo, Tavus 25 min/mo, Clay 500 actions/mo. None roll over. A burst of research in week one silently pushes week four onto paid rails. Your cost estimator MUST read live remaining quota, not assume the monthly headline, or it will under-quote exactly when you are busiest.
- TURKEY PAYMENT AND FX FRICTION — most of these vendors bill in USD with card-only checkout; Turkish cards face 3D-Secure friction, FX spread and the BSMV/KKDF levies on FX card transactions, plus reverse-charge KDV obligations on imported digital services for a Turkish company. Prefer vendors with no subscription floor (HeyGen wallet, Bright Data PAYG, Exa credits) so you are not committing TRY-denominated recurring spend against a volatile rate.
- SELF-HOSTED PRESENTON SHIFTS COST TO YOU, NOT AWAY — 'free lane' with local Ollama means local GPU/CPU time, model management and slower turnaround. Measure actual wall-clock time for a 12-slide Turkish deck locally before declaring it the default; if it takes 8 minutes and the premium lane takes 40 seconds, the premium lane is correct for a pre-meeting deadline and your cost model should surface TIME alongside money.
- OVER-PERSONALIZATION READS AS AUTOMATED — decks stuffed with scraped facts about the prospect trigger suspicion rather than flattery, especially in Turkish B2B where relationships are personal and a machine-assembled dossier can feel invasive. Cap personalization at a handful of high-signal fields and keep a human approval gate before anything reaches a prospect. This is a commercial risk, not just an aesthetic one.
- STALE RESEARCH SHIPPED AS FACT — a dossier assembled three weeks before a meeting may cite a departed executive or a closed tender. Stamp every research file with fetched_at and have the deck pipeline refuse to run, or loudly warn, on sources older than a configurable threshold (14 days suggested). Quoting a wrong fact about a prospect to their face is a deal-killer that no amount of design polish recovers from.
