## Özet

Two things changed the landscape by mid-2026. First, OpenAI exited: the Sora consumer app was killed in April 2026 and the Videos API + all sora-2 model IDs are formally deprecated with a hard shutdown on 24 September 2026, with no successor named. Build nothing on Sora. Second, per-second prices collapsed. Google Veo 3.1 Lite is $0.05/sec at 720p with native audio, LTX-2.3 is $0.06/sec at 1080p with audio, Grok Imagine Video is $0.05/sec, and Vidu Q3 Turbo is $0.065/sec at 1080p (roughly half that off-peak). A 10-second branded clip with sound now costs 50-80 US cents, not $5.

The quality frontier is ByteDance Seedance 2.5 (30 s native, up to 30 image + 10 video + 10 audio references in one call — the best character/brand consistency available), Kling 3.0 (multi-shot, native audio, ~$0.11-0.20/sec on fal), Runway Gen-4.5 ($0.12/sec flat), and Veo 3.1 (first+last frame, 3 reference images, +7 s extends up to 20x, 4K).

Open weights are alive but narrower: LTX-2.5 (free commercially under $10M revenue, native audio), MiniMax-H3 (33B, audio, but its licence needs an application and covers only US/EU/UK/KR), Wan 2.2 + Wan-Animate-2 (Apache 2.0), Kandinsky 5 (MIT, runs in 12 GB). Alibaba's Wan 2.5/2.6/2.7 are API-only.

For a brand system, the bigger news is the composition layer: HeyGen's HyperFrames (Apache 2.0, 40.9k stars) renders agent-written HTML/CSS to deterministic MP4, and Higgsfield ships an MCP server plus MIT-licensed Claude Code skills for brandkit, character ID and explainer videos.

<details><summary>Notlar</summary>

**Higgsfield (platform + Cloud API + MCP + CLI)** — MCP uses your subscription credits, not separate API billing — that is the cheap path. Top-up credit packs reportedly expire after 90 days (use-it-or-lose-it). Outputs inherit the underlying model's terms, not Higgsfield's, so Veo/Kling restrictions still apply. No public SLA or rate-limit table. Company is Higgsfield Inc.; the 4k-star `higgsfield-ai/higgsfield` repo is an unrelated older GPU-training framework.

**higgsfield-ai/skills (Claude Code agent skills)** — MIT licence permits commercial and private use. Skills are prompts, so read them before trusting — they encode Higgsfield's opinions about your brand, which you will want to override with your own brand-system files.

**HyperFrames (HeyGen)** — Requires Node.js 22+ and FFmpeg. 40.9k stars / 3.9k forks — this is the real repo. IMPORTANT GOTCHA: `github.com/hyperframes/hyperframes` (2 commits, 1 star) and the site hyperframes.app are NOT HeyGen; that site sells paid cloud rendering for the Apache-2.0 project and returns 403 to bots. Use heygen-com/hyperframes. No dedicated MCP server (skills instead).

**Google Veo 3.1 / 3.1 Fast / 3.1 Lite (Gemini API)** — Veo 3 (non-3.1) models were shut down 30 June 2026 — migrate any old code. All outputs carry a SynthID watermark. Generated videos are deleted from Google's servers after 2 days, so download immediately. CRITICAL FOR TURKEY: the API enforces regional restrictions on generating people in EU, UK, CH and MENA — confirm whether your billing/region is treated as MENA before designing person-centric pipelines. Lite does not support 4K or video extension.

**OpenAI Sora 2 / Sora 2 Pro (Videos API)** — VERIFIED deprecation entry covers Videos API, sora-2, sora-2-pro, sora-2-2025-10-06, sora-2-2025-12-08, sora-2-pro-2025-10-06. The consumer Sora app was discontinued 26 April 2026. Even while alive it rejected human faces in input images, blocked real people/public figures, and blocked copyrighted characters and music — hostile to a brand pipeline anyway.

**Runway Gen-4.5 (+ Aleph 2.0, Act-Two) via Runway API** — Only buy Gen-4.5, Aleph and Act-Two here; buy Veo from Google and Seedance from ByteDance/fal/Pika. Aleph's 56-credit minimum makes short clips disproportionately expensive.

**ByteDance Seedance 2.5** — The reference budget is the killer feature for a brand system: feed logo, product shots, prior frames and a voice sample in one call. Seedance 2.0 was API-gated at launch (enterprise early access) — verify your account can actually call 2.5 before committing. Higgsfield claims exclusive 1080p Seedance 2.5 access, which is UNVERIFIED.

**Kling 3.0 / 3.0 Omni (Kuaishou)** — klingai.com blocks bots and geo-varies; the global site (kling.ai) and the Chinese domestic site have different prices in CNY vs USD. Buy through fal or Pika to avoid CN payment friction from Turkey. Multi-shot is the differentiator over Veo/Runway for storytelling.

**MiniMax H3 (Hailuo) — API** — Enterprise pay-as-you-go plus prepaid video packs; individual/team token subscriptions also exist. Being both API and open-weight is rare and useful for cost control.

**MiniMax-H3 (open weights)** — CRITICAL: the MiniMax H3 Community License Agreement requires an application for commercial use, and that application is available for USA / EU / UK / South Korea ONLY. A Turkey-based operator has no listed route to commercial rights. Use the paid API instead, where terms are contractual, or pick LTX-2.5.

**LTX-2.5 (Lightricks, open weights)** — The <$10M revenue carve-out makes this the safest open licence for a solo Turkish operator. fp8 casting + CPU offload enable low-VRAM deployment. Note the licence conflict: fal's LTX-2.3 page claims Apache 2.0 while the HF card says LTX-2.x Community License — trust the model card. Fine-tune redistribution is the one clause to watch.

**LTX-2.3 / LTX-2 Pro (hosted on fal)** — URGENT: the fal-ai/ltx-2 (LTX-2 Pro) endpoint is deprecated on 15 August 2026 — tomorrow. Point new code at fal-ai/ltx-2.3. The LoRA hook is what makes this viable for character consistency across a whole content calendar without paying reference-budget prices.

**Luma Ray3.2 (Dream Machine API)** — 540p at $0.03/sec is genuinely cheap for storyboard/animatic passes. HDR + EXR export is unique and matters only if you are compositing in a real NLE. No native audio mentioned in the pricing docs.

**Pika (dev.pika.art)** — Big surprise of 2026 — Pika is now infrastructure, not a model. Bundling video, image, TTS, music, lipsync and upscaling behind one key genuinely reduces pipeline complexity. The 5% platform fee is already baked into quoted prices. Single-vendor concentration risk given how fast Pika repositioned.

**fal.ai** — Rate-limits aggressive automated fetching (429s), so cache model metadata rather than scraping at runtime. Model pages explicitly mark 'Commercial use permitted'. Endpoints get deprecated with ~weeks of notice (see LTX-2 → LTX-2.3), so pin model IDs and monitor changelogs.

**Replicate** — Prices are usually higher than fal or Pika for the same model — use Replicate for discovery and A/B, then move the winner to the cheapest host. You can also push your own Cog images if you want to self-host LTX or Wan on their GPUs. Model pages often omit price in the rendered markdown; read the price from the API.

**Freepik API (now docs.magnific.com)** — Freepik's real value is stock + Magnific upscaling bundled with generation, not frontier video. The docs.freepik.com → docs.magnific.com redirect suggests an in-progress rebrand; expect endpoint churn. Verify output licensing carefully — Freepik's stock licence terms and model output terms are different things.

**AI/ML API** — Useful only if you specifically want one OpenAI-compatible key across LLMs and media. Their docs expose an `?ask=` GitBook query param for programmatic doc search, which is handy. Prefer fal or Pika for video.

**Segmind** — Dedicated endpoints with auto-scaling are the real differentiator if you ever want a warm LTX or Wan instance. 60 RPM on the entry tier. Rate card sits behind the model pages, not the pricing page.

**Vidu (Q3 Pro / Q3 Turbo / Q2 / S1)** — Off-peak pricing is a real, underused lever for an automated content calendar: schedule tomorrow's Reels tonight. Start-end-to-video is the cleanest first+last-frame control of any commercial API. Voice cloning in the same platform means one vendor for Turkish VO + video, though Turkish TTS quality needs its own test.

**Grok Imagine Video (xAI)** — Audio support is NOT documented in the xAI video guide despite third-party claims of synced audio — verify before relying on it. All output is subject to content-policy review. Rate limits are not published. Reputationally the loosest content policy of the majors, which cuts both ways for a brand account.

**PixVerse v5.6 / API** — Effects+templates are the fastest path to trend-format Reels without prompt engineering, which suits a solo operator chasing formats. Renders in ~10 s, the fastest in this list. Anime/animation is its documented standout, so a poor fit if your brand is photoreal SaaS.

**Adobe Firefly Services (video)** — Firefly video generation exists in the Adobe apps but I could not verify a public video API endpoint from primary docs (multiple generate_video URL patterns 404). Adobe's genuine differentiator is IP indemnification and training-data provenance, which matters if you ever sell client work — but that is an enterprise contract conversation, not an API key.

**HeyGen API (avatars, translation, HyperFrames)** — v1/v2 endpoints are supported only through 31 October 2026 — start on v3. Turkish is very likely covered by the 30+ translation languages but I could not verify the exact list; test with real Turkish copy before committing. HeyGen also authors HyperFrames, so the free renderer and the paid avatar service compose cleanly.

**sync.so (lipsync)** — Input video should be English-spoken; the audio track can be any language, which is exactly the Turkish-dubbing case. sync-3 is native 4K; lipsync-2 and 2-pro cap at 512x512, so check resolution before you commit to a model. Max duration up to 30 minutes depending on plan. Commercial terms are not spelled out in the docs — read the ToS.

**Wan 2.2 (Alibaba, open weights)** — 17.1k stars, last updated March 2026. Apache 2.0 is the cleanest licence of any capable open video model — no application, no region limit, no revenue threshold. CRITICAL: Wan 2.5, 2.6 and 2.7 are NOT open — github.com/Wan-Video/Wan2.7 returns 404 and huggingface.co/Wan-AI's newest uploads are Wan2.2-Animate-2, not 2.7. Replicate's Wan 2.7 page cites Apache 2.0, which contradicts this; treat that as boilerplate until Alibaba publishes weights.

**Wan-Animate-2 / Wan-Dancer (Alibaba)** — Released 7 August 2026 — one week old, so expect rough edges. Defaults are tuned for 8x A800 GPUs at 720p; 480p tested on 2x A800. That is rental-GPU territory, not a laptop. No audio generation. Wan-Dancer-14B is the sibling image-to-video motion model.

**HunyuanVideo-1.5 (Tencent)** — 14 GB minimum means a 4080/4090 or a cheap cloud GPU can run it — the lowest hardware bar of the capable open models. No native audio. Tencent's Hunyuan licences have historically carried restrictions (region and MAU clauses on some releases) — READ LICENSE.txt in the repo before commercial use; the repo page does not state the terms. Released November 2025 with no 2026 successor announced.

**Kandinsky 5.0 Video (Sber / ai-forever)** — Runs on 12 GB GPUs with VAE tiling and offloading. Distilled variants give ~6x speedup (35 s for a 5 s clip). No audio generation. Documented strength is 'Russian concepts' in prompts — Turkish prompt adherence is untested and probably weak, so prompt in English. Released Sept-Nov 2025.

**CogVideoX / CogVideoX1.5-5B (Zhipu)** — Only reason to pick this in 2026 is if you are stuck on a 6-8 GB GPU. The 5B licence is custom, not Apache — check it. No audio. CogKit framework landed March 2025 and things have been quiet since.

**Mochi 1 (Genmo)** — Apache 2.0 is clean, but that is its only remaining advantage. Documented weaknesses: struggles with animated/stylised content, warping under extreme motion, optimised for photorealism only. Listed here for completeness.

**ComfyUI** — 127.5k stars. LICENCE WARNING: GPL-3.0, not MIT — fine for personal/self-hosted use, but if you ever ship a product embedding ComfyUI you inherit copyleft obligations. Workflow JSON is the natural 'brand template' artefact for an agentic system. API nodes send data to third parties; disable them if you care about that.

**Remotion** — THE licence is the whole story: free below 4 people, and you must upgrade the moment you hire. Budget for that if the Creative Suite ever becomes a business. Deterministic React rendering is ideal for templated brand videos, animated decks and data explainers where exact typography and colour matter more than AI 'creativity'. Pairs well with a Turkish-TTS voiceover track.

**Revideo** — 4.0k stars, 1,143 commits, actively developed. MIT with NO employee threshold, which is the argument for choosing it over Remotion if you expect to grow. Anonymously reports render events to PostHog by default — set DISABLE_TELEMETRY=true. Smaller ecosystem and fewer components than Remotion, so expect to build more yourself.

**Motion Canvas** — 18.9k stars, actively maintained (955 commits, 151 open issues). The voice-over sync workflow is genuinely excellent for product explainers. But if the agent must render unattended, Revideo (its automation-focused descendant) or HyperFrames is the better pick — Revideo exists precisely because Motion Canvas was hard to automate.

**Editly** — 5.5k stars, 430 commits, no deprecation notice. ESM-only, needs a current Node LTS. This is the right tool for the boring-but-essential final assembly step: concatenate three Veo clips, crossfade, duck the music under the Turkish VO, letterbox to 9:16. Same author as LosslessCut.

**Shotstack API** — 4K is High-Volume-only, which rules it out for premium brand assets on a solo budget. The sandbox environment is genuinely useful for iterating templates without burning credits. Strong choice if you refuse to run render infrastructure; otherwise HyperFrames does the same job for the cost of electricity.

**Creatomate API** — Genuinely good if you want a visual template editor a non-developer can touch — but that is not this user's constraint. The data-feed feature (1,000-100,000 rows) is built for bulk personalised ads, which only pays off at volume. No free tier, only a trial.

**JSON2Video** — Explicitly does NOT include AI image generation, AI video generation, avatars or face-swap — it is pure composition, so you still need Veo/LTX/Kling upstream. Pre-paid credits not expiring makes it the best 'occasional use' hosted renderer. Refunds only within 24 h of first payment and under 10% credit use. Test its Turkish TTS voices before relying on them.

**FFmpeg pipelines** — LICENCE NUANCE: a build linked against libx264/libass is GPL, which matters only if you redistribute binaries — irrelevant for personal self-hosted use. Practical wins for this project: `loudnorm` to normalise Turkish VO to -14 LUFS for social platforms, `sidechaincompress` to duck music under narration, and libass subtitle burn-in so Turkish captions survive platforms that ignore sidecar SRT files.

### Öneriler

- Build the pipeline on a two-layer split: use AI generation only for footage that must look filmed (product beauty shots, lifestyle B-roll, ambient Reels backgrounds), and use deterministic HTML/React rendering — HyperFrames or Remotion — for anything containing your logo, typography, brand colours, UI screenshots, pricing tables or Turkish on-screen text, because diffusion models will never render Turkish diacritics or your exact hex codes reliably.
- Make Higgsfield's MCP server (https://mcp.higgsfield.ai/mcp) your default generation entry point and fork higgsfield-ai/skills (MIT) as the skeleton for your command-center agent, since it already ships brandkit, soul-id character training, product-photoshoot and video-explainer skills that map almost one-to-one onto your brief — then replace its brand assumptions with your own brand-system files.
- Adopt HyperFrames (Apache 2.0, 40.9k stars, heygen-com/hyperframes) as the templated-video engine and pull only from that repo — the hyperframes.app site and the github.com/hyperframes/hyperframes mirror are unaffiliated third parties selling paid cloud rendering for a free Apache-2.0 project.
- Write zero code against OpenAI Sora: the Videos API and every sora-2 model ID shut down on 24 September 2026 with no announced successor, so if any existing prototype or tutorial you are copying uses POST /videos, discard it now.
- Tier your model spend explicitly in the agent's config — Veo 3.1 Lite ($0.05/sec) or LTX-2.3 ($0.06/sec at 1080p with audio) for the three-to-five angle proposals the user picks from, then Seedance 2.5 or Kling 3.0 for the single approved hero shot — which keeps a 10-shot exploration under a dollar instead of over ten.
- Use Seedance 2.5 whenever a shot must carry brand or character continuity, because its 30-image + 10-video + 10-audio reference budget in a single call is the only mechanism on the market that reliably holds a product, mascot or colour system consistent across an entire content calendar; Veo 3.1 caps at 3 reference images and Kling 3.0 at multi-image reference.
- Schedule your batch renders against Vidu Q3 Turbo during off-peak hours, where 13 credits/sec at 1080p ($0.065/sec) drops roughly 50% to about $0.033/sec — for a solo operator producing tomorrow's posts overnight this is the single largest cost lever available and requires only a cron job.
- Verify before you build whether Google's Veo regional restriction on generating people in EU/UK/CH/MENA applies to a Turkey-based billing account, and design a fallback (Kling 3.0 or Seedance via fal/Pika) into the pipeline from day one rather than discovering it mid-campaign.
- For self-hosting, pick LTX-2.5 as the default and Wan 2.2 as the LoRA workhorse: LTX-2.x's Community License grants free commercial and production use under $10M annual revenue with native audio, and Wan 2.2 is plain Apache 2.0 with the deepest LoRA ecosystem — but avoid MiniMax-H3's open weights entirely, since its commercial-use application is available only to US/EU/UK/South Korea entities and offers a Turkey-based operator no compliant route.
- Handle the Turkish-language problem as a separate stage rather than a prompt: generate video with English prompts (every model's training skews heavily English), then attach Turkish voice-over via a TTS service and, if a face is on screen, re-sync it with sync.so's lipsync-2 at $0.02-0.13/sec — sync.so explicitly supports an English-spoken source video with an audio track in any language, and offers 3 free generations a month to test with.
- Keep Remotion in the stack while you are solo — its licence is free for individuals and companies up to 3 people including commercial use — but record in your project docs that hiring a fourth person triggers a $25/seat/month or $100/month+$0.01/render obligation, and evaluate Revideo (MIT, no headcount clause) if that day looks likely.
- Pin every model ID and subscribe to each provider's changelog, because endpoint churn is now the main operational risk: fal's LTX-2 Pro endpoint deprecates on 15 August 2026, Google's Veo 3 models were killed on 30 June 2026, HeyGen's v1/v2 endpoints end on 31 October 2026, and Sora ends on 24 September 2026 — all within a single year.


### Riskler

- Endpoint churn is the dominant risk in this domain right now — four separate shutdowns land within twelve months (Sora 24 Sep 2026, fal LTX-2 Pro 15 Aug 2026, Veo 3 30 Jun 2026, HeyGen v1/v2 31 Oct 2026). Any pipeline that hardcodes model IDs without an abstraction layer will break repeatedly.
- Google's Veo API enforces regional restrictions on generating people in EU, UK, CH and MENA. Whether a Turkey-based account falls under MENA is unverified and could silently break person-centric campaigns.
- MiniMax-H3's open-weight Community License limits commercial-use applications to USA, EU, UK and South Korea — a Turkey-based operator has no listed compliant path to commercial rights on the open weights, despite the weights being freely downloadable.
- Licence contradictions are common and dangerous: fal's LTX-2.3 page states Apache 2.0 while the Lightricks HF model card states the LTX-2.x Community License with a $10M revenue cap; Replicate's Wan 2.7 page cites Apache 2.0 while no Wan2.7 repo or HF weights exist. Always trust the model card / repo LICENSE file over an aggregator's marketing copy.
- Higgsfield publishes no fetchable API rate card, no billing docs page, and no SLA. The widely-quoted $0.10/sec is from listicles, not a primary source — you will not know real costs until you fund a Cloud account, and its top-up credits reportedly expire after 90 days.
- Payment and availability from Turkey is untested across every vendor here: Chinese platforms (Volcengine, Kling domestic, Vidu) frequently require CN payment rails or corporate verification, and some US providers geo-restrict. Route through fal.ai, Replicate or Pika where possible to keep a single payment relationship.
- ComfyUI is GPL-3.0, not MIT. Fine for personal self-hosted use, but embedding it in anything you later distribute or sell inherits copyleft obligations — a real trap given the user's 'not a product to sell (for now)' framing.
- Aggregator markups are large and easy to miss: Runway charges $0.36-0.40/sec for Seedance 2 versus ByteDance's ~$0.14/sec direct, and $0.40/sec for Veo 3.1 versus Google's $0.40 with no discount. Discovery on Replicate then execution on the cheapest host is the only sane pattern.
- Named-person and copyright guardrails vary wildly and are enforced inconsistently — Sora rejected human faces in input images outright, Veo has regional person restrictions, and Grok has the loosest policy. A brand pipeline that assumes a face reference will always be accepted will fail unpredictably mid-run.
- Google deletes generated videos from its servers after 2 days and all Veo output carries a SynthID watermark. Any pipeline that does not download and archive immediately will lose assets, and watermarked output may matter for some client or platform contexts.
- Wan-Animate-2 was released 7 August 2026 (one week before this research) and is tuned for 8x A800 GPUs — treat every claim about it as provisional and budget for rental GPUs, not local hardware.
- CogVideoX and Mochi are effectively abandoned (last substantive releases November 2024) yet still rank highly in search results and tutorials. Following a 2024-era open-source video tutorial in 2026 will waste days.
