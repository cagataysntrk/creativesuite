## Özet

As of Aug 2026 you should not build the pipeline layer yourself — three categories have consolidated hard. (1) Publishing: Postiz (34.7k, AGPL-3.0, pushed 2026-08-14) is the clear winner with a real REST API, a NodeJS SDK, an n8n node and a separate agent CLI/skill (gitroomhq/postiz-agent) that plugs straight into Claude Code. BrightBean Studio (2.1k, AGPL-3.0) is the only rival that ships a first-party MCP server. Mixpost (MIT) is the licence-safest but its Lite repo has slowed (last push 2026-03-16). (2) Video: the surprise of 2026 is calesthio/OpenMontage (48.1k, AGPL-3.0) — an "agentic video production system" that is literally YAML pipelines + 700 markdown skill files + a Python tool registry, driven from your coding agent's chat. That is architecturally the same thing you were about to build, and it explicitly supports a zero-paid-API path (Piper TTS, Archive.org footage, local Remotion). MoneyPrinterTurbo (103.4k, MIT) remains the best batteries-included topic→vertical-video REST API. OpenShorts (3.1k, MIT core) covers long→short repurposing and ships an MCP server. ShortGPT is dead (last push 2025-02-10) — do not touch it. (3) Decks/graphics: hugohe3/ppt-master (46.8k, MIT) and iOfficeAI/OfficeCLI (28.3k, Apache-2.0, MCP + no Office install) make native .pptx a solved problem; Presenton (Apache-2.0) gives you a self-hosted REST+MCP deck service. For rendering brand-consistent stills, ComfyUI (GPL-3.0) + SwarmUI (MIT) + vercel/satori (MPL-2.0, HTML/CSS→SVG) beat any "OSS Canva" — the real Canva clones are toys and Polotno is not open source at all ($249/mo floor). Licence reality: almost everything good is AGPL-3.0, which is harmless for personal self-hosted use but poisons any future productisation.

### Riskler

- Licence contagion is the dominant risk: the best projects in nearly every category (Postiz, OpenMontage, BrightBean, ArcReel, Khoj, SiYuan, Trilium, Logseq, Docmost, guizang-ppt-skill) are AGPL-3.0, which is fine for personal self-hosted use but permanently blocks a closed-source or hosted-product path unless you keep your code strictly separate and communicate only over network APIs.
- Hidden non-commercial licences on model weights, not code: Coqui XTTS-v2 ships under CPML which prohibits commercial use, and many ComfyUI custom nodes and diffusion checkpoints carry their own restrictive terms — the repository licence you check is often not the licence that governs the assets you actually ship.
- Repositories with no licence file at all (Anil-matcha/AI-Youtube-Shorts-Generator at 4,591 stars, OpenCMO, ALwrity at 1,119 stars and pushed today) are legally all-rights-reserved; forking them is copyright infringement regardless of how open they look.
- Star counts are actively misleading in this space: Flowise is archived at 55,364 stars, Quivr is 13 months stale at 39,398 stars, Fooocus is 8 months stale at 52,235 stars, and several 20k-50k-star video repos gained their stars in months on a few hundred commits — audit `archived`, `pushed_at`, commit count and open-PR backlog before trusting any of them.
- Browser-automation publishing (MultiPost-Extension) violates the automated-access terms of Meta, LinkedIn and TikTok and can get your accounts restricted or banned; the README does not disclose this, and account loss would take your whole distribution channel with it.
- Even with Postiz or BrightBean handling transport, you must still register and get approval for your own Meta, TikTok, LinkedIn and Google developer applications — for a solo operator in Turkey this approval process (especially Instagram Content Publishing and Google Ads developer tokens) is a real, multi-week, occasionally-rejected bottleneck that no open-source project removes.
- Turkish-language quality is unverified across nearly the entire stack: TTS voice quality, Whisper transcription accuracy on smaller models, marketing-skill idiom, and font glyph coverage for ğ/ş/ı/İ/ç/ö/ü in Satori and Remotion templates all need explicit testing before any of these tools go into production.
- Heavy dependence on Chinese-first projects (Pixelle-Video, Toonflow, ArcReel, PostBot, pyJianYingDraft, MoneyPrinterTurbo's issue tracker) means documentation lag, platform assumptions aimed at Douyin/Xiaohongshu rather than Instagram/LinkedIn, and slower support when something breaks.
- Open-core pressure on the fastest-moving projects (Cap, InvokeAI, CrewAI, Khoj, Dify, Composio, Activepieces, LiteLLM) means today's free feature can become tomorrow's enterprise-tier feature; Dify already restricts multi-tenant operation and logo removal, Open WebUI restricts white-labelling above 50 users, and n8n's Sustainable Use License is not an open-source licence at all.
- Remotion's Free License evaporates the moment you exceed three employees, which converts a free foundational dependency into a per-seat cost at exactly the point where you are scaling and least able to rewrite your video templates.
- Assembling ten-plus self-hosted services (Postiz, ComfyUI, Presenton, AnythingLLM, LibreChat, LiteLLM, LocalAI, a TTS service, a knowledge base and an orchestrator) creates a substantial and unglamorous operations burden — upgrades, credential rotation, disk, GPU scheduling and backups — that will consume the time you meant to spend on content.
- GPU dependency is understated across the video and image stack: OpenShorts self-hosted on CPU takes 5-8 minutes per 8-minute video versus roughly 50 seconds on a GPU, and ComfyUI, Chatterbox and F5-TTS all effectively assume one — meaning the 'free' path has a real hardware or rented-GPU cost.
