## Özet

Research method note: this session's WebSearch budget was exhausted before I started, so all findings below come from direct WebFetch of primary/official sources plus DuckDuckGo-HTML result pages used as a search substitute, and two PDFs extracted locally with pdftotext. Everything I could not confirm from a primary source is listed in `unverified`.

The headline: 2026 is the single best content year Turkish industrial software will get for a decade, because four independent compliance clocks all land on the same buyer at once. (1) CBAM entered its definitive regime on 1 January 2026; Regulation (EU) 2025/2083 exempts 90% of importers via a 50-tonne de-minimis while still covering 99% of embedded emissions, moved certificate sales to 1 February 2027 and the annual declaration deadline to 30 September. Practical effect: Turkish steel and cement exporters must produce verifiable, plant-level embedded-emission data during 2026 and pay for it in 2027. (2) Turkey's own Climate Law No. 7552 (in force July 2025) starts an ETS pilot in 2026-2027 with 100% free allocation, covering ~41% of national emissions, with mandatory MRV — installations above 50,000 tCO2. (3) TSRS scope was *narrowed* on 16 January 2026 (now 1bn TL assets / 2bn TL revenue / 500 employees, two of three, two consecutive periods) — so the mandatory-reporting TAM shrank, and the real demand is now supply-chain pull, not statutory obligation. (4) CSRD's Omnibus finished on 24 February 2026 at 1,000 FTE + €450m turnover, with an explicit value-chain cap stopping in-scope EU customers from over-asking suppliers — which kills the "your German customer will force you" pitch and replaces it with a competitive-advantage pitch.

The commercially sharpest finding is not a regulation: KOSGEB's KOBİ Dijital Dönüşüm Destek Programı requires an approved TÜSSİDE-DDX or MEXT/İHKİB-SIRI digital-maturity report before a NACE-C manufacturer can access 1-20m TL of interest-subsidised credit. That is a fully-funded, government-mandated diagnostic step sitting immediately upstream of every software purchase — the natural wedge for a solo custom-solutions house.

Buyer reality (6sense 2025): 10+ person buying groups, 10.1-month cycles, buyers engage sellers 61% through the journey, and the Day-One favourite wins 95% of the time. You cannot out-sell that late; you can only be on the Day-One list, which is a publishing problem, not a sales problem.

**CBAM definitive regime (Regulation (EU) 2023/956 as amended by 2025/2083)** — Verified from the Commission's CBAM pages plus ICAP's summary (https://icapcarbonaction.com/en/news/eu-adopts-simplifications-cbam-rules-ahead-compliance-phase-starting-2026). EUR-Lex blocked both WebFetch and curl (202/empty), so article numbers are UNVERIFIED. Content angle: the 2027 payment date makes 2026 the year to install measurement — 'you cannot invoice-back a number you did not measure'. **Turkey's CBAM trade exposure (World Bank index methodology)** — Extracted locally with pdftotext. Turkey's high EAF/scrap share is explicitly named as the reason steel's carbon gap is smaller than feared. This is a superb contrarian content hook: 'Turkish aluminium is cleaner than the EU benchmark — CBAM is an export weapon for you, if you can prove the number.' Secondary blogs claim ~41% of Turkey's EU exports are CBAM-covered and ~€8-19bn exposure; those figures are UNVERIFIED.

**Türkiye Emisyon Ticaret Sistemi (ETS) + İklim Kanunu No. 7552** — Verified from the Ministry of Environment/Climate site. This is the most under-exploited content topic in Turkey: it converts carbon from an export-compliance annoyance into a domestic operating cost with an allocation benchmark. 'Benchmark-based free allocation' means intensity per unit of output — which is exactly an MES/energy-monitoring data problem, not a spreadsheet problem.
