## Özet

For Turkish marketing audio in mid-2026 the picture is unusually clear: ElevenLabs is still the quality leader (Eleven v3 covers 70+ languages including tr, Multilingual v2 $0.10/1k chars, Flash/Turbo $0.05/1k, free tier 10k credits/mo), and its Scribe STT claims 3.8% WER on Turkish FLEURS versus 7.5% for Whisper large-v3 — the single most useful verified Turkish datapoint found. The credible cheap alternatives are Google Gemini TTS (gemini-3.1-flash-tts-preview, Turkish confirmed in docs, $1/1M text-in + $20/1M audio-out, and it has a free tier) and Cartesia Sonic-3.5 (tr confirmed in the API language list, free 20k credits, commercial licence from $5/mo). Azure is a trap for Turkish: only two plain neural voices (AhmetNeural, EmelNeural), no HD, no styles. Hume Octave, Kokoro, Orpheus, Sesame CSM, VibeVoice and IndexTTS-2.5 have no Turkish at all. Self-hosted, the only genuinely usable Turkish stack is Chatterbox Multilingual V3 (MIT, tr in the 23-language list, Perth watermark baked in), Piper (three medium-quality tr_TR voices, near-zero cost) and XTTS-v2 (17 langs incl. tr, but weights are CPML non-commercial with no one left to sell a licence). Music is the weak link: Suno still has no official public API, Udio's status is unconfirmed, and the only clean commercially-cleared music APIs are ElevenLabs Music v2 ($0.15/min) and Beatoven maestro. For captions, Groq Whisper large-v3-turbo at $0.04/hr plus ffmpeg/libass or Remotion (free under 3 people) is effectively free and fully automatable.

### Riskler

- Vendor Turkish claims are almost never backed by published Turkish benchmarks — the only Turkish-specific accuracy number found anywhere in this research is ElevenLabs' own 3.8% FLEURS WER for Scribe, which is self-reported. Every 'supports 200+ languages' claim (Inworld, Fish Audio, Speechify) must be audited by ear before you trust it with brand content.
- Turkish is agglutinative: single roots generate hundreds of valid suffixed forms, which stresses TTS vocabulary coverage and produces the mid-word stress errors and mispronounced long compounds that plague models trained mostly on Common Voice Turkish. Expect to maintain a pronunciation-override lexicon for your product names, brand terms and any English loanwords in your copy.
- Licence contamination is the most likely way this project produces a legal problem: XTTS-v2 (CPML), F5-TTS checkpoints (CC-BY-NC), MMS-TTS Turkish (CC-BY-NC), MMAudio checkpoints (CC-BY-NC), AudioCraft weights (CC-BY-NC) and Fish-Speech (custom research licence) are all free to download and all forbid the commercial use your marketing content constitutes. Running them on Replicate or fal.ai does not launder the licence.
- Music rights are the sharpest edge. Mubert explicitly forbids Content ID registration, standalone streaming release and stock-site redistribution on standard plans and retains ownership of all generated music; Suno's commercial grant evaporates if your subscription lapses; Freesound requires case-by-case negotiation with UPF for commercial API use. A single mislicensed background track can trigger a platform copyright strike on an account you depend on.
- Pixabay's API terms explicitly prohibit systematic mass downloads and automated querying and mandate 24-hour result caching — an agentic asset-fetching loop is exactly the behaviour they ban, and their music library has no documented API endpoint at all.
- Several vendors in this space are unstable. play.ht and play.ai both failed DNS resolution during this research; Coqui is dead; Udio's post-UMG status is unconfirmed; Resemble's public pricing has pivoted to deepfake detection; Orpheus and Sesame CSM show no commits since 2025. Do not make any single vendor load-bearing without a documented fallback path.
- Preview-tier model instability: Google's TTS models are all still labelled 'preview' (gemini-3.1-flash-tts-preview, 2.5-flash-preview-tts), ElevenLabs v3 shipped as alpha, and Hume Octave 2 is preview. Model IDs and pricing on these can change or be withdrawn with short notice — pin model IDs in config, not in code, and monitor changelogs.
- Synthetic-voice disclosure and watermarking: Chatterbox embeds Perth watermarks in every output, ElevenLabs free-tier dubs are watermarked, and D-ID watermarks Trial/Lite output. Separately, platform policies (and increasingly EU/Turkish regulation on AI-generated content labelling) may require you to disclose synthetic voice and avatar content in ads — check current rules before running paid campaigns with a cloned voice.
- Cost blow-up from iteration, not volume: ElevenLabs Dubbing v2 at $2.20/min, HeyGen Avatar V at $4.00/min and Sync.so at $3.00/min are all priced per output minute with no discount for regeneration. An agent that proposes five angles and renders all five turns a 60-second Reel into a $20 line item. Put a hard credit ceiling and a human approval gate in front of every per-minute-billed API.
- Turkish word-level alignment for karaoke captions is a real gap: WhisperX ships alignment models only for en/fr/de/es/it by default, so Turkish requires sourcing a third-party wav2vec2 Turkish checkpoint whose own licence you must verify separately. Budget engineering time for this or route around it via ElevenLabs Scribe's character-level timestamps.
- Data handling: free tiers on Gemini API and several other providers may use submitted content for product improvement. If your scripts contain unreleased product details, bespoke client information or strategy documents from your Creative Suite knowledge base, route those through paid tiers with the relevant no-training terms, or through self-hosted models.
