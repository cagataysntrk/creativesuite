## Özet

As of August 2026 the publishing landscape splits cleanly into three tiers for a one-person operation. Tier 1 — you can ship code today with zero review: Telegram Bot API, Bluesky/AT Protocol, Mastodon, Discord webhooks, and (critically) Meta's own surfaces. Meta's App Review page explicitly states that an app serving only a business you own or manage runs on Standard Access with "App Review: Not required", and Business Verification is only required for Advanced Access or when other businesses use your app. So Instagram (feed/carousel/Reels/Stories), Facebook Pages and Threads are all reachable from your own code with your own Meta app — the single biggest misconception in the ecosystem. LinkedIn personal profiles are also self-serve: the "Share on LinkedIn" product grants w_member_social without partner approval (150 requests/member/day); only Company Pages and analytics need the vetted Community Management API, which is closed to individuals. Tier 2 — reachable but gated by an audit you must survive: TikTok Content Posting API (unaudited clients are locked to SELF_ONLY on private accounts, max 5 users/24h), YouTube Data API (uploads from unverified projects are silently locked to private; only 100 videos.insert/day by default), and Pinterest (Trial access pins are sandbox entities visible only to you). Tier 3 — pay to play: X killed the free tier on 2026-02-06 and moved to pay-per-usage credits at $0.015 per post, jumping to $0.200 if the post contains a URL. Almost nothing offers native scheduling — only Facebook Pages (scheduled_publish_time, 10 min–30 days) and Mastodon (scheduled_at) — so you self-schedule everything else with your own queue. For the aggregation layer, self-hosted Postiz (AGPL-3.0, 32 platforms, REST API + MCP) is the best free option, with Ayrshare/upload-post as paid escape hatches when a platform audit blocks you.

**Instagram Content Publishing API (Instagram Graph / Instagram API with Instagram Login)** — Rate limit: 100 API-published posts per rolling 24h; a separate line in the same doc caps carousel posts at 50/24h — query GET /{ig-user-id}/content_publishing_limit for the account's real number. JPEG is the ONLY supported image format (no PNG, no MPO/JPS). media_type=REELS and media_type=STORIES are supported. Carousels: up to 10 items, aspect ratio taken from the first image (default 1:1). No shopping tags, no filters via API. alt_text field added 2025-03-24 for images only. NO native scheduling — you must run your own queue. Accounts requiring Page Publishing Authorization (PPA) cannot be published to until PPA is completed. Long-lived tokens expire in 60 days and must be refreshed.

**Meta App Review + Standard vs Advanced Access** — The doc's own table states: use case 'My app is only for a business I own or manage' + 'No login or Instagram Login' + 'Standard Access' → 'App Review: Not required'. Business Verification is separately confirmed as required only when an app 'requires advanced level access to permissions' or lets OTHER businesses access their data — explicitly 'not needed if your app is exclusively used by people holding roles directly on the app'. Practical consequence: add your own Meta account as Admin/Developer on your own app and you publish to your own IG/FB/Threads with no review, no company, no screencast. Advanced Access also requires at least 1 successful API call before you can even submit.

**Threads API (graph.threads.net)** — 250 published posts per profile per rolling 24h (carousel = 1 post); replies capped separately. Text limited to 500 chars (emoji counted as UTF-8 bytes). Images JPEG/PNG, max 8MB, width 320–1440px, sRGB, max 10:1 aspect. Video MOV/MP4, H264/HEVC, moov atom at front, 23–60 FPS, ≤100 Mbps, ≤300s, ≤1GB, 9:16 recommended. Carousel 2–20 items. Max 5 links, and links only on text-only posts. Wait ~30s after container creation before publishing. No native scheduling.

**Facebook Pages API (publishing)** — NATIVE SCHEDULING: set published=false + scheduled_publish_time (Unix seconds, ISO 8601, or strtotime strings like '+2 weeks'). Hard window: between 10 minutes and 30 days from the request. Your app can only update posts it created. Note Meta has progressively de-ranked pure link posts; put the link in the first comment if reach matters.

**LinkedIn — Share on LinkedIn product (w_member_social)** — Rate limits published on the page: 150 requests per member per day (UTC reset), 100,000 per application per day. Media flow is 3-step: registerUpload (images/video/documents endpoints on the versioned API), PUT the binary to the returned uploadUrl, then reference the asset URN in the post. NO native scheduling. Access tokens are typically 60 days; refresh tokens require an approved product for some apps — plan for periodic re-auth. Requires the app to be associated with a LinkedIn Page you admin (a personal 'brand' page is enough to create the app).

**LinkedIn Community Management API (Company Pages, member analytics)** — Development Tier rate limits were raised to 500 req/app and 100 req/member. r_member_social is a CLOSED permission — LinkedIn states 'We're not accepting access requests at this time due to resource constraints', which kills programmatic reading of your own post analytics. Note the Marketing 202507 version has been sunset; you must track LinkedIn's monthly versioning (LinkedIn-Version header) or your integration breaks.

**X (Twitter) API v2** — CRITICAL COST DESIGN RULE: never put a URL in the primary post — a 13x price multiplier. Put links in a reply or in the bio. Reads are deduplicated within a 24h UTC day. Auto-recharge fires at most once every 5 minutes. Payment from Turkey requires an international card; budget in USD. No native scheduling. Zernio offers pass-through X billing at cost if you'd rather not hold X credits directly.

**TikTok Content Posting API (Direct Post)** — THE BIG BLOCKER: unaudited clients can only post SELF_ONLY, all posting accounts must be set to private at post time, and max 5 users can post per 24h. Audit takes 2–4 weeks with multiple feedback rounds. Audit UX requirements are hard gates: you must display the creator's nickname/avatar, fetch creator_info before every post, present a privacy selector with NO default (values must come from privacy_level_options), expose Comment/Duet/Stitch toggles, collect the commercial-content/branded-content disclosure, and warn the user about processing delay. Rate limits: 6 init requests/min per user access_token; upload_url valid 1 hour; ~15 posts/day per creator shared across ALL API clients. Caption max 2,200 UTF-16 chars. PULL_FROM_URL requires domain ownership verification. A fully automated, no-UI agent pipeline structurally cannot pass this audit — you need a real approval screen.

**YouTube Data API v3 (videos.insert)** — THE SILENT KILLER: videos uploaded via videos.insert from an API project created after 2020-07-28 that has NOT passed the compliance audit are permanently locked to private, and re-uploading through the API will not fix it. Quota (current docs wording): 'a default quota allocation of 100 search.list calls, 100 videos.insert calls, and 10,000 units per day combined for all other endpoints'. Playlist insert/update/delete = 50 units each. Quota extension and the private-lock removal both require the same audit (https://developers.google.com/youtube/v3/guides/quota_and_compliance_audits). Native scheduling DOES exist via status.publishAt when privacyStatus=private. Shorts are just ≤3-min vertical uploads — no separate endpoint.

**Pinterest API v5** — TRIAL ACCESS IS A SANDBOX: per Pinterest's own docs, 'all Pins and Boards created with Trial access are only visible to their creator as Sandbox entities'. Trial is also rate limited per day per app (~1,000 requests/day). Standard access is per-minute per-user per-app (roughly 1,000/min read, 100/min write, 60/min trends). Upgrading to Standard requires a video demo showing the OAuth flow and the integration in use, with no sensitive data on screen — a several-week review with a meaningful first-pass rejection rate. Your pins will not be publicly visible until Standard is granted.

**Bluesky / AT Protocol (com.atproto.repo.createRecord)** — 300 grapheme character limit. Images: up to 4 per post, ~1MB each after upload via com.atproto.repo.uploadBlob; you must set alt text and aspectRatio yourself. Rich text facets (links, mentions, hashtags) must be byte-index computed manually — the API does NOT auto-link. Rate limits are a points system (widely cited as 5,000 points/hour and 35,000/day with createRecord = 3 points, i.e. ~1,666 posts/hour) — I could not load the canonical rate-limits page this session, treat the exact numbers as UNVERIFIED. No native scheduling. Self-hostable PDS if you want full sovereignty.

**Mastodon API (POST /api/v1/statuses)** — NATIVE SCHEDULING via scheduled_at (must be ≥5 minutes in the future); returns a ScheduledStatus entity you can list/cancel. Media flow: POST /api/v2/media then pass media_ids[]. Polls and media are mutually exclusive. Use Idempotency-Key header to avoid duplicate posts on retry. Rate limits are per-instance (typically 300 req/5 min general, 30 statuses/30 min on mastodon.social) — instance-dependent, so verify against your chosen home.

**Telegram Bot API** — Supports MarkdownV2/HTML formatting, link previews on/off, silent notifications, and editing/deleting posts after the fact. Webhooks support up to 40 simultaneous connections by default (configurable 1–100). Broadcast throttling (commonly cited ~30 messages/second overall, ~20 messages/minute to a single group) is enforced but not stated in the section I could load — treat the exact numbers as UNVERIFIED. Telegram Bot API is also the cheapest way to get push notifications back to yourself from your pipeline.

**Reddit Data API** — 60 requests/minute for OAuth2 clients; obey X-Ratelimit-Used / -Remaining / -Reset headers. User-Agent MUST follow <platform>:<app ID>:<version> (by /u/<username>) — spoofing gets you banned outright. The real risk is not the API: subreddit moderators and Reddit's own anti-spam will shadowban an account that posts self-promotional or AI-generated content on a schedule. Karma/age gates block new accounts from most large subs. Reddit's Data API terms restrict commercial and AI-training use — read them before building anything beyond personal posting. Also note automated posting to r/* is the one place where an obviously scheduled brand cadence is actively punished by humans.

**Discord Webhooks** — Rate limit is per-webhook (commonly 5 requests per 2 seconds, 30/min) — UNVERIFIED against the docs this session. Supports rich embeds, custom username/avatar per message, and file attachments up to the server's upload limit. Excellent as your pipeline's ops log and as a community channel for a Turkish-language product audience.

**WhatsApp Business Platform (Cloud API)** — Requires Meta Business Verification (real legal entity, documents) — one of the few surfaces a private individual genuinely cannot get. Every marketing template needs prior approval and opt-in proof; unsolicited broadcast will get the number quality-rated down and eventually blocked. WhatsApp Channels have no public publishing API as of this research.

**Postiz (self-hosted)** — LICENCE: AGPL-3.0 — fine for personal/self-hosted use; if you ever expose a modified Postiz over a network to others you must publish your source. Create-post endpoint rate limit: 90 req/hour self-hosted (100 cloud), tunable via the API_LIMIT env var — batch multiple posts per request. IMPORTANT CAVEAT: self-hosting does NOT bypass platform gatekeeping — for most platforms you register your OWN developer app and set the keys as env vars, so you still face the TikTok audit, Pinterest Standard upgrade and YouTube compliance audit yourself. Ten platforms need no app at all (Bluesky, Lemmy, Nostr, Medium, Dev.to, Hashnode, WordPress, Listmonk, Moltbook, Skool). Postiz sells optional paid 'app approval' assistance — a signal of how painful the reviews are.

**Mixpost** — Licence is NOT OSI-open for Pro/Enterprise: you may modify the source for exclusive internal business use but may not redistribute or sell modified code, and Pro explicitly may not be used to build a SaaS. Perpetual fallback licence included. Same caveat as Postiz: you still supply your own platform developer apps. Compare carefully against free Postiz — the main reasons to pay are Laravel familiarity and the one-time-fee model.

**Ayrshare** — The genuine value is that Ayrshare has already passed TikTok's audit, Pinterest Standard review, YouTube compliance audit and Meta app review — you never touch those. Best-documented API in the category. Historically had a free tier with a watermark; that appears gone. Check whether their TikTok integration exposes the required privacy selector — you are still bound by TikTok's UX rules downstream.

**Zernio (formerly Late / getlate.dev)** — For your realistic footprint (IG + LinkedIn + X + TikTok + YouTube + Threads ≈ 6 accounts) you'd pay roughly $24/mo and skip every platform audit. Verify per-platform capability depth (Stories? carousels? documents?) before committing — unified APIs usually support the lowest common denominator. The Late→Zernio rebrand means old getlate.dev docs/URLs redirect; pin the new base URL. Note their blog is heavily SEO-driven, so trust the pricing page over the blog.

**upload-post.com** — Documented constraints: Instagram comments support replies only, not top-level comments; LinkedIn analytics limited to organization pages (not personal profiles); TikTok has no public comments API; AutoDM is Instagram-only, 2 monitors per profile/day, 15-day expiry. Built-in FFmpeg job API is genuinely useful for a creative pipeline (re-encode a generated Reel to platform spec server-side). Launched Jan 2025 — younger and less battle-tested than Ayrshare; do not make it your only path to a critical channel.

**Blotato** — API is gated behind a paid plan (not available during trial), so you cannot evaluate the part you actually need for free. 'AI credits' map roughly to one generated image or a few seconds of video — you are paying a markup on models you can call directly. Only 9 platforms vs Postiz's 32. Their blog produces a lot of the 'API pricing' listicles that dominate search results for this topic — treat those as marketing, not primary sources.

**Buffer Public API (2026)** — This is a genuine change: Buffer's old API was closed to new developers for years. Buffer has done all the platform app reviews. Determine whether API access requires a paid channel plan before designing around it. Verify supported post types per channel (Stories, Reels, carousels, LinkedIn documents) — Buffer historically lagged on these.

**Publer API** — Notably wide post-type coverage: text, images, video, GIF, carousels, Reels, Stories, polls, PDFs. Bulk-schedule up to 500 posts in one operation, plus recurring and recycled content — genuinely useful for an evergreen Turkish-content library. Platforms: Facebook, Instagram, X, LinkedIn, Pinterest, YouTube, TikTok, Google Business, WordPress, Telegram, Mastodon, Threads, Bluesky. Rate limits not published — probe before relying on burst posting.

**Metricool + MCP server** — Metricool's strength is competitor benchmarking and analytics rather than publishing throughput. If you want analytics fed back into your agent's angle-selection step, this is the most likely candidate — but verify the API/MCP plan gate first. The repository URL I tried (github.com/metricool/mcp-metricool) 404'd during this research; locate the canonical repo before installing.

**n8n (self-hosted Community edition)** — Licence is the Sustainable Use License (fair-code, NOT OSI open source): free for internal business use, but you may not host it as a service for third parties or white-label it. Personal self-hosted use is squarely permitted. Paid-only features in Community: SSO, environments, projects, external secrets, workflow sharing — none of which a solo operator needs. Dedicated first-party social nodes are thin (X, Telegram, Reddit, Mastodon, Discord); for Instagram/Threads/LinkedIn/TikTok you will use the HTTP Request node or the Postiz community node.

**Zapier** — The free tier's two-step limit makes it useless for propose→approve→generate→publish flows. Webhooks-by-Zapier being paid-only removes the one reason a developer would use it. Keep it in mind only as a last-mile connector to something with no API.

**Make.com** — Make renamed 'operations' to 'credits' — 1,000/month is genuinely enough for a solo posting cadence. Unlike Zapier, the free plan includes HTTP + webhooks, so it can actually talk to arbitrary APIs. Still hosted-only, so your brand system and credentials live on someone else's infrastructure — contrary to your self-hosted goal.

**Google Business Profile API** — Posts expire after 7 days unless they are events/offers, so this rewards a recurring automated cadence — an ideal target for your agent. Requires a verified business location. Not a 'social' network but arguably higher conversion than Instagram for bespoke-software leads.

**Nostr (NIP-01 kind 1 notes)** — No review, no rate limit beyond per-relay policy, no ToS to violate. Zero risk of deplatforming. Realistically a rounding error in reach for Turkish-language business content, but it is the purest example of the 'no gatekeeper' tier and a useful architectural reference for your publisher abstraction.

**Dev.to / Hashnode / Medium APIs** — Always set canonical_url to your own site so syndication does not cannibalise your SEO. Dev.to rate limit is roughly 10 article creations per 30 seconds. Medium's API has been effectively frozen for years — do not build on it. These are the cheapest way to give an agent-generated explainer a permanent home that the social posts can point to.

**WordPress REST API** — Native scheduling, native media library, full control, no ToS risk, no deplatforming risk. This is the only 'platform' in this list that cannot revoke your access. Architecturally: your agent should write here first, then fan out to social — which also solves the X $0.20-per-link problem (link in a reply pointing to your own domain).

**Instagram content_publishing_limit endpoint** — Meta's own content-publishing doc contains both a '100 posts per 24-hour moving period' statement and a '50 published posts within 24 hours' statement, and developers report throttling below both. This endpoint resolves the ambiguity per account. Build it into your publisher as a pre-flight check and back off on 429/quota exhaustion.

**Meta long-lived token refresh flow** — The most common failure mode for solo self-hosted publishers: everything works for two months, then all Instagram/Facebook/Threads posts fail at once. Schedule a refresh at ~day 50 and alert yourself via Telegram when refresh fails. Also handle Meta's periodic forced re-auth after password changes or permission revocations.

### Öneriler

- Build a single internal Publisher interface with one adapter per platform (publish(draft) -> {url, id}) and treat native scheduling as an optional capability, because only Facebook Pages (scheduled_publish_time, 10 min to 30 days), Mastodon (scheduled_at, min 5 minutes) and YouTube (status.publishAt) support it — everything else you must self-schedule with your own cron/queue in Europe/Istanbul time (UTC+3, no DST since 2016).
- Register ONE Meta app, add your own account as an app Admin, and stay on Standard Access — Meta's App Review doc explicitly says review is 'Not required' when the app only serves a business you own, and Business Verification is only triggered by Advanced Access; this single decision unlocks Instagram feed/carousel/Reels/Stories, Facebook Pages and Threads for free with no company, no screencast and no 2-4 week wait.
- Add LinkedIn's self-serve 'Share on LinkedIn' product to a LinkedIn app to get w_member_social and publish to your personal profile today (150 requests/member/day), and do NOT attempt the Community Management API for a Company Page — it is a vetted product requiring a legal entity, a two-tier review and a screencast, and r_member_social is closed entirely.
- Start Phase 1 with the five zero-gatekeeper platforms — Telegram, Bluesky, Mastodon, Discord and your own WordPress — to prove the whole agentic loop (angle proposal, user pick, generation, approval, publish) end to end before you spend a single day on an app review; the entire Phase 1 stack costs 0 TL and can ship this week.
- Self-host Postiz (AGPL-3.0, Docker, 32 platforms, REST API at 90 create-post requests/hour, plus an MCP server and an n8n node) as your scheduling backbone rather than writing 12 adapters yourself — but budget for the fact that self-hosting does not bypass platform gatekeeping, since for most networks you still supply your own developer app keys as environment variables.
- For the three audit-gated platforms (TikTok Content Posting, YouTube videos.insert, Pinterest v5), do not attempt the audits as a solo operator on your first pass — route them through Zernio (first 2 connected accounts free, then $6/account/month) or upload-post.com (free tier plus an open-source MCP server), both of which have already passed those reviews, and revisit doing it yourself only if per-account costs become material.
- Complete the YouTube API compliance audit BEFORE you upload anything programmatically: videos uploaded via videos.insert from an unverified project are permanently locked to private and re-uploading through the API does not fix it, and the default quota of 100 videos.insert calls/day is fine for you but the private-lock is not.
- Never put a URL in the primary body of an X post — X's published pay-per-use rate is $0.015 per post but $0.200 per post containing a link, a 13x multiplier; publish the post text alone and put your link in a self-reply, which also happens to be better for X's reach algorithm and keeps a 30-posts/day cadence at roughly $13.50/month instead of $180.
- Put a mandatory human approval step between generation and publishing, surfaced as a Telegram bot message with inline Approve/Reject/Regenerate buttons — this costs nothing, is the only realistic way to keep Turkish-language brand voice quality high, and is also what TikTok's audit requirements and Meta's platform terms effectively assume (a creator who sees and confirms the content and its privacy level before it goes out).
- Store every generated asset on a public HTTPS URL (S3-compatible bucket, Cloudflare R2 or your own VPS behind a CDN) before publishing, because Instagram, Threads and TikTok's PULL_FROM_URL all fetch media by URL rather than accepting a multipart upload — and note that TikTok additionally requires you to verify domain ownership before it will pull from your domain.
- Normalise media at generation time to the strictest common denominator per format: JPEG only for Instagram images (PNG is rejected outright), sRGB, 1080x1350 for feed, 1080x1920 9:16 for Reels/Stories/TikTok, H.264 MP4 with the moov atom moved to the front for every video surface — and consider upload-post.com's FFmpeg jobs API or a local ffmpeg step to enforce this automatically.
- Call GET /{ig-user-id}/content_publishing_limit as a pre-flight check before every Instagram publish, and build a token-refresh job that runs around day 50 of the 60-day Meta long-lived token lifecycle with a Telegram alert on failure — these two pieces of plumbing prevent the two most common silent failures in self-hosted Meta publishing.


### Riskler

- Meta's Standard Access path is your whole Instagram/Facebook/Threads strategy and it rests on the app remaining 'only for a business you own' — if you ever let a second person authenticate against your app, or Meta reinterprets the policy, you fall into Advanced Access which requires Business Verification with real legal-entity documents. Mitigate by keeping the app strictly single-tenant and having a fallback aggregator (Zernio/upload-post) configured but idle.
- TikTok's audit is structurally hostile to a fully automated agent: the requirements mandate showing the creator's avatar and nickname, fetching creator_info before every post, presenting a privacy selector with no default drawn from privacy_level_options, and exposing Comment/Duet/Stitch toggles. A headless pipeline cannot pass. Unaudited you are capped at SELF_ONLY posts on private accounts with 5 users per 24h — effectively unusable.
- Pinterest Trial access creates Pins as sandbox entities visible only to you. It is entirely possible to build, test and 'successfully' publish a hundred Pins that no one on Pinterest can ever see. Verify visibility from a logged-out browser before you trust any Pinterest integration.
- X's pay-per-use model has no free tier and no spend floor protection beyond your credit balance — a retry loop or a runaway agent could burn credits fast, especially at $0.200 per link-bearing post. Set a hard daily post cap in your own code, not just in the X console, and disable auto-recharge until the pipeline is proven.
- Payment and availability friction from Turkey: X API credits, Ayrshare, Blotato and Buffer all bill in USD and require an international card; some providers geo-restrict or apply extra KYC. WhatsApp Business additionally requires Meta Business Verification with legal-entity documents that a private individual cannot supply.
- Postiz is AGPL-3.0. Personal self-hosted use is completely fine, but the moment you expose a modified instance over a network to anyone else — a client, a collaborator, a public demo — you are obligated to publish your modifications, which would include any brand-system integration you built into it. Keep your brand/product code in a separate service that talks to Postiz over its REST API rather than forking Postiz.
- Platform terms increasingly require disclosure of AI-generated media (Meta's AI labelling, TikTok's AIGC label) and several platforms treat undisclosed synthetic content as a violation. Bake an is_ai_generated flag into your content model and set the platform's AI-content field on every generated image or video.
- Reddit is the highest deplatforming risk in the list: the API is trivially easy but moderators and anti-spam systems actively punish scheduled self-promotional and AI-generated posts, and account bans there are usually permanent. Treat Reddit as manual-only or extremely low-cadence.
- Multi-platform aggregators expose the lowest common denominator of each platform's capabilities — Stories, LinkedIn document carousels, Instagram collabs, Threads carousels and TikTok photo posts are commonly missing or degraded. Verify each specific post format you care about against the aggregator before you architect around it, or keep direct adapters for your two or three highest-value formats.
- LinkedIn versions its Marketing/Community APIs monthly and sunsets versions aggressively (202507 is already sunset); /v2/ugcPosts and /v2/shares are deprecated and receive no new features. Any LinkedIn code you write needs the LinkedIn-Version header pinned and a calendar reminder to migrate, or it will break without warning.
- Several of the most attractive commercial options are very young or just rebranded — Late became Zernio in 2026, upload-post launched Jan 2025, Buffer's public API is brand new. Pricing, URLs and even company existence are less stable here than the platform APIs themselves; do not let a startup aggregator be your only path to a channel that matters.
- The search results for this entire topic are dominated by vendor-authored SEO content (Blotato, Zernio, Postproxy, Netrows and similar all publish near-identical 'API pricing 2026' listicles). Numbers in those posts frequently contradict the platforms' own docs. Always re-verify against developers.facebook.com, docs.x.com, developers.tiktok.com and learn.microsoft.com before committing a number to your cost model.
